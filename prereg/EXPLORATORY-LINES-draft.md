## Tier 1: zero GPU, register now as exploratory

E1. **Item-level decoupling (the "silent regression" split).** For each confirmed-regressed derivative, the items it newly complies with (base refused, derivative complied) are scored by the per-item change in the base-direction projection, as a fraction of the base's harmful-minus-harmless gap (continuous, margin-normalised; a binary split at the 1%-FPR threshold is reported but does not decide, because that threshold sits far below the harmful projections on the easy contrast). Primary statistic: within-derivative decision-tracking AUROC (projection against the derivative's own J1 refusal label on the H batteries and the Karne ladders), paired against the base's on the Karne ladders (the base has enough compliance there). Rule: the direction has stopped reading the refusal decision if the median paired drop exceeds 0.10 with a base-stratified lower bound above 0. Prior work to position against: Messenger 2608.05578 (model-level class), Karne 2609.05797 (H5), Que 2609.34970, and Zhao et al. 2507.11878 (harmfulness and refusal encoded separately; harmfulness read near the instruction-final token), which predicts part of this at p* = eoi-9, so the last-token line is co-primary here.
E2. **Compute-matched black-box canary baseline for D2.** A behavioural canary of the same cost as D2 (the J1 refusal rate on a seeded 64-item subsample of the H batteries, read from the existing outputs) as a predictor of confirmed regression, paired-bootstrap AUROC difference against D2. If the canary matches D2, the white-box score has no cost advantage and the paper says so.
E3. **Operating-point rules for D2.** The H2(a) threshold fixed by a label-free rule (the base-vs-base noise floor times a pre-stated factor) and by leave-one-base-out calibration; report recall, false-alarm rate and the share of behavioural re-evaluation a deployer could skip, per base, with the regressions each rule misses. Underpowered with four bases; reported as a deployer appendix.
E4. **Cross-distribution transfer of drift.** Whether drift measured on the contrast set predicts refusal loss on distant prompt distributions (each Karne explicitness level, XSTest unsafe), per condition. Zero GPU.
E5. **Shrink versus rotate.** Whether fine-tuning reduces the projection gain along a fixed refusal subspace or rotates the direction (CPU linear algebra on cached activations), as the mechanistic reading of D1 versus D2 and of why mean-shift realignment works or fails.
E6. **Where to read.** Staleness (delta-AUROC) at every captured (position, layer) cell against detection at that cell; whether the Arditi-selected cell is the most fine-tuning-robust one. Zero GPU if the drift stage stores all cells on the test split; otherwise a prefill-only re-capture (about 3 H200-h per base).

## Tier 2: small compute, highest main-track value (amendment candidates)

E7. **Causal mediation on regressed units.** On confirmed-regressed derivatives plus two non-regressed controls per base: (a) restore the base projection along r at inference, (b) remove the adapter's write along r, (c) the same along the persona vectors, each against 10 random directions, judged at 512 tokens. Prediction: r restores refusal after benign-data regressions, persona directions after trait and insecure-code regressions. About 6 to 8 H200-h per 9B base. Detection-only use; nothing saved. This is the causal leg that turns H2 and H3 from correlations into mechanism.
E8. **Matched-data dissociation.** One secure-code LoRA per base (Betley's secure control data, same recipe, same size as condition 4) so that drift can be tested against harm at equal data distance: does D2 separate insecure-code from secure-code derivatives? About 4 H200-h per 9B base including batteries. Answers the reviewer's "drift only detects that fine-tuning happened".
